\begin{abstract}
Finding the most efficient order to multiply a chain of matrices---the Matrix Chain Multiplication (MCM) problem---isn't just a classic textbook exercise anymore. Today, it is a critical bottleneck sitting at the heart of modern deep learning compilers and database query optimizers. The standard way to find this optimal order uses Dynamic Programming (DP), which takes $O(n^3)$ time. In latency-sensitive systems, taking cubic time just to \textit{plan} the math before actually \textit{doing} the math is simply too slow.

In this paper, we ask a provocative question: Can we skip the expensive $O(n^3)$ search process entirely and use a neural network to instantly predict the optimal cost in constant $O(1)$ time?

To answer this, we built a sequence-to-sequence Pointer Network that learns the underlying structural logic of the problem, rather than just guessing numbers. We then benchmarked it against heavy-duty statistical models (XGBoost and Random Forest) using 120,000 highly irregular, stress-testing matrix chains.

The results were striking. While the standard machine learning models were fast, they failed to understand the mathematical physics of the problem, predicting impossible costs over 85\% of the time. Our Pointer Network, however, achieved a \textbf{100\% Mathematical Validity Rate}---meaning every single prediction it made was physically possible. On top of being perfectly safe, it achieved near-perfect accuracy, with error rates dropping to 0.59\% on standard workloads and 0.02\% on structured chains. Ultimately, this work proves that we can safely replace slow, classical search algorithms with instant neural inference to dramatically speed up computational graph planning.
\end{abstract}
